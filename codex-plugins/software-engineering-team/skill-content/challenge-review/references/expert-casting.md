# Expert Casting

How to invent the right practitioners for a topic and get proposals,
not hallucinated facts, out of them.

## Writing a profile

A castable profile is one paragraph with four parts:

1. Identity: role and seniority in the domain's own vocabulary ("a
   warehouse operations lead who has run physical inventory counts for
   fifteen years"), never a generic consultant.
2. Scars: two or three failure modes this person has lived through;
   scars aim the expert at what actually goes wrong.
3. Incentive: what this person is measured on; it predicts which gaps
   they will care about.
4. Blind spot: what this profile predictably overweights; triage reads
   their findings knowing it.

Cast 2 to 4 profiles per domain, orthogonal to each other: an operator
(does the work daily), a controller (audits or reconciles it), and where
relevant a counterpart (the upstream or downstream role the process
hands off to). DON'T cast two profiles that would give the same answer.

## Worked example (inventory domain)

"A cost accountant at a mid-size distributor, twelve years closing
monthly books. Scars: a physical count that never reconciled because
goods-receipt postings crossed the period boundary; a write-off battle
caused by untracked damaged stock. Measured on close time and audit
findings. Blind spot: will trade operator convenience for auditability
every time."

## What to send an expert

- The named questions (OQ ids and their rows), the domain subtree read
  fully, the glossary and registry summary-only. Never the conversation.
- Questions of owner PREFERENCE ("should we support consignment?") are
  not expert questions; send only questions of domain KNOWLEDGE ("what
  do distributors typically do when a receipt exceeds the order?").

## Reading the answers

- Every proposal lands as an assumption row (source: the profile) or a
  proposed resolution attached to its open question; the owner's ruling
  at the gate converts, adjusts or strikes it.
- An expert answering outside its profile, or with certainty language on
  an unlabeled claim, loses the point: re-ask or drop, never transcribe.
- Two experts disagreeing is signal, not noise: record both positions on
  the open question and let the owner rule.
